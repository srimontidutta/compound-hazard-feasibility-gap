# CHFG specification

This specification collects the definitions used by the paper and the reusable records in this repository.

## 1. Scenario-conditioned plan feasibility

Let \(H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_m\}\) be recognized hazards or induced disruptions. A scenario \(S\subseteq H\) has realized operational state \(\omega_S\). A subset \(H_G\subseteq H\) contains the hazards that activate protection goals and constituent response plans. For a scenario \(S\),

\[
T(S)=S\cap H_G
\]

is the set of response-triggering constituents.

A response plan contains actions \(a\in A\), protection goals \(G\), binary operational capabilities \(k\in K\), and renewable resources \(r\in R\). Capability availability is represented by

\[
z_k(\omega)\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Each action requires a set of capabilities \(K(a)\). For renewable resource \(r\), let \(C_r(t,\omega)\) be available capacity and \(d_r(a,t)\) the demand generated by action \(a\). For a schedule \(\pi\), concurrent demand is

\[
D_r(\pi,t)=\sum_{a\in A_t(\pi)} d_r(a,t),
\]

where \(A_t(\pi)\) is the set of actions active at time \(t\).

Define \(F(\pi,\omega)=1\) when required capabilities remain available during execution, renewable-resource demand does not exceed capacity at the relevant times, and the required protection goals are achieved. Otherwise \(F(\pi,\omega)=0\).

For each response-triggering hazard \(h\in H_G\), let \(\pi_h\) be its constituent response. A declared merge rule \(\mu\) produces the default combined response

\[
M_S=\mu\!\left(\{\pi_h:h\in T(S)\}\right).
\]

The merge rule preserves the internal temporal and precedence constraints of each constituent response and fixes any cross-response timing or synchronization choices needed for a well-defined feasibility check. A plan with explicit alternatives may define a permitted response set \(\mathcal R(S)\) containing the default composition and the alternatives allowed for the active protection goals.

## 2. Eligible compound scenarios

CHFG conditions on successful constituent checks. Let \(\mathcal C\) be the evaluated compound-scenario suite. The eligible set is

\[
\mathcal C^+=\left\{S\in\mathcal C:T(S)\neq\varnothing\;\land\;F(\pi_h,\omega_{\{h\}})=1\;\forall h\in T(S)\right\}.
\]

Conditioning on successful constituent checks attributes the resulting gap specifically to the compound composition of those responses. Constituent responses that already fail in their single-hazard states remain visible through the separately reported constituent coverage.

## 3. Compound-Hazard Feasibility Gap

For strictly positive analysis weights \(w_S>0\),

\[
\mathrm{CHFG}=
\frac{\sum_{S\in\mathcal C^+}w_S\,\mathbb I[F(M_S,\omega_S)=0]}
{\sum_{S\in\mathcal C^+}w_S}.
\]

With uniform weights, CHFG is the fraction of eligible stress-test scenarios that lose default feasibility after composition. When weights are defensible joint scenario probabilities for a specified planning distribution or climate horizon, the weighted quantity has a conditional-probability interpretation over the evaluated scenario class.

Consequence-sensitive analyses can be reported separately when defensible impact information is available.

## 4. Contingency coverage

Using the same eligible set,

\[
\mathrm{CC}=
\frac{\sum_{S\in\mathcal C^+}w_S\,\mathbb I[\exists\pi'\in\mathcal R(S):F(\pi',\omega_S)=1]}
{\sum_{S\in\mathcal C^+}w_S}.
\]

CHFG measures the share of constituent-valid compound scenarios in which the default response loses feasibility. Contingency coverage measures the share for which at least one permitted response remains feasible.

## 5. Failure diagnosis

For a failed default response, retain independent capability and resource indicators:

\[
P(S)=\mathbb I[\exists a\in A(M_S),\,k\in K(a):z_k(\omega_S)=0],
\]

\[
Q(S)=\mathbb I[\exists r,t:D_r(M_S,t)>C_r(t,\omega_S)].
\]

A failed scenario can therefore carry a capability witness, a resource witness, or both. These witnesses connect the diagnostic to plan revision: capability failures point toward dependency substitution, hardening, or alternative access, while resource contention can motivate rescheduling, prioritization, mutual aid, pre-positioning, or added capacity.

## 6. Reporting

A CHFG result is interpreted together with its constituent coverage and evaluated scenario family. A compact report should include:

- constituent single-hazard coverage;
- the evaluated compound-scenario family and weighting rule;
- CHFG and its denominator size or probability mass;
- contingency coverage on the same eligible set;
- capability and resource witnesses for failed default scenarios;
- protection goals that remain unmet after permitted alternatives are evaluated.

Comparisons between plans are most informative when the protection goals, compound scenarios, dependency semantics, and weighting rules are compatible.

## 7. Terms used in the repository

**Action.** An operation used by a response plan to advance a protection goal.

**Capability.** An operational condition required by an action, represented as binary in the paper's base formalism. Examples include road access, usable facilities, communications, and grid connectivity.

**Renewable resource.** A simultaneously constrained capacity whose active demands are compared with available capacity over time. The illustrative case uses fuel-service availability, response-team capacity, and mobilization capacity as normalized service-capacity classes.

**Constituent response.** The response maintained for a single response-triggering hazard.

**Default combined response.** The response produced by the declared merge rule when the relevant constituent responses are activated together.

**Eligible compound scenario.** A compound scenario that activates at least one protection goal and whose relevant constituent responses pass their corresponding single-hazard feasibility checks.

**Compound-Hazard Feasibility Gap (CHFG).** The weighted share of eligible compound scenarios in which the default combined response is infeasible.

**Contingency coverage.** The weighted share of eligible compound scenarios for which at least one permitted response remains feasible.

**Failure witness.** A disabled required capability, an overloaded renewable resource at a relevant time, or both, retained to explain a failed default composition.

**Stress-severity multiplier.** The sensitivity parameter used in the illustrative case to scale the listed capacity decrements while baseline capacities and binary capability states remain fixed.
